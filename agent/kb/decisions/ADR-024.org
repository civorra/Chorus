#+TITLE: ADR-024 — origin / direction / evidence classification model in chorus-compare-kb
#+DATE: 2026-11-18
#+STATUS: accepted
#+DOMAIN: skills
#+ENG:

* Context

=chorus-compare-kb= produces a cross-sandbox comparison matrix used by legal
and compliance experts who need to know not only *whether* two normative corpora
diverge, but *where* the divergence lies, *which side is stricter*, and *how
confident* the finding is.

Without a formal classification, matrix rows risk mixing three fundamentally
different situations:

1. A concept covered in corpus A but simply absent from corpus B's KB
   (a *coverage gap*, not necessarily a legal divergence).
2. A genuine legal difference: same concept, same field, different threshold,
   scope, or obligation level.
3. A terminological mismatch resolved by the thesaurus (no real divergence).

Conflating these in a single "divergence" label produces misleading reports
and wastes domain-expert review time.  Additionally, consumers of the report
(lawyers, regulators) need to know which side is stricter and whether the
finding is text-anchored or LLM-inferred — two distinct levels of confidence.

* Decision

Every row in a =chorus-compare-kb= comparison matrix carries B<three mandatory
classification fields>:

** origin — nature of the row

| Value | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| =kb-gap= | Concept covered in one corpus but absent from the corresponding KB |
| =legal-divergence= | Concept present in both KBs with substantive difference (threshold, scope, obligation) |
| =thesaurus-mismatch= | Same concept, different terminology — resolved by the thesaurus |
| =structural= | Agent or Frame present in one sandbox but absent in the other |
| =uncertain= | Requires human review before classification |

Invariant: a one-sided row (normative atom on only one side) can B<never> be
labelled =legal-divergence=.  It stays =kb-gap= until Phase 4.3 rules out the
provision in the other corpus (=absence_proof= filled).  Upgrading a
=kb-gap= to =legal-divergence= requires atoms on both sides.

** direction — asymmetry of the divergence (legal-divergence rows only)

| Value | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| =A_stricter_than_B= / =B_stricter_than_A= | Same obligation, tighter on one side |
| =A_weaker_than_B= / =B_weaker_than_A= | One side grants less protection or imposes less |
| =different_structure= | Different mechanism, not comparable on a strict/weak axis |
| =undetermined= | Cannot be ordered without a legal opinion |

When one corpus is a minimum-harmonisation instrument (e.g. an EU directive) and
the other its national implementation, direction is expressed relative to that
instrument.

** evidence — confidence level of the finding (legal-divergence rows only)

| Value | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| =text-anchored= | Both atoms cite distinct texts; difference computed deterministically |
| =witness-confirmed= | Phase W (=chorus-stress= boundary tests) produced differing verdicts |
| =witness-artefact= | Phase W produced identical verdicts — textual difference, no behavioural one |
| =llm-only= | LLM-assisted alignment, not corroborated by text or witness |

=text-anchored= and =witness-confirmed= are the two strongest levels and are
never collapsed into a single "confirmed" label.

* Positive consequences

- Domain experts reviewing the matrix can immediately filter by =origin= to
  skip coverage gaps and focus on genuine legal divergences.
- The =direction= field provides the legal asymmetry ("A is stricter") in a
  machine-readable form, enabling automatic aggregation in =--report= output.
- The =evidence= field distinguishes deterministic findings from LLM inferences,
  giving the reviewer a calibrated trust level per row.
- The model is extensible: new =origin= values can be added without breaking
  the existing classification logic.

* Negative consequences / accepted trade-offs

- Every matrix row requires three fields instead of one; the authoring burden
  per row is higher.
- =direction= and =evidence= apply only to =legal-divergence= rows — their
  presence on other row types is undefined and ignored.
- =llm-only= findings require human validation before they can be used in a
  legal report; the model does not enforce this — only the =--report= output
  workflow does.

* Rejected alternatives

| Alternative | Reason for rejection |
|-------------|----------------------|
| Single "divergence level" score (1–5) | Mixes confidence and legal severity; unreadable by domain experts |
| Free-text "notes" per row | Not machine-readable; =--report= cannot aggregate |
| Merge =text-anchored= and =witness-confirmed= into one "confirmed" level | Loses the distinction between textual and behavioural proof — legally different |
| Make =direction= optional on =legal-divergence= rows | A divergence without direction is incomplete for legal analysis; forces a human classification pass that could be done at matrix creation time |
